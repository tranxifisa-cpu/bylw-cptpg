\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Endogenous reference formation changes a CPT portfolio problem at two levels at once.  It changes the economic state being optimized, because identical current wealth can be evaluated differently after different gain--loss histories, and it changes the learning problem, because the resulting objective moves with both market conditions and the investor's own realized path.  The results connect these two levels within one first-order framework: a frozen dynamic-reference target admits statistically controlled gradient estimation, and the online update can track the sequence of moving first-order conditions through the projected residual and dynamic local regret.

This connection extends two strands of prior work that have largely been treated separately.  Fixed-reference CPT reinforcement learning establishes how nonlinear probability weighting can be optimized with policy gradients \citep{prashanth2016cumulative,lepel2026policygradients}, while adaptive-reference models in behavioral finance show that realized gains and losses can shift the benchmark against which subsequent outcomes are judged \citep{arkes2008reference,he2019realization,qin2022realization,gao2024multiperiod}.  DRCPT-PG combines these ideas by making the reference an endogenous optimization state.  E1 makes the consequence concrete at the decision level, and E3--E4 show that once the target moves with that state, continued first-order updating reduces the accumulation of tracking error across a broad range of reference dynamics, horizons, and optimizer scales.

The market and behavioral studies indicate that the same mechanism remains informative beyond the controlled construction.  In the chronological A-share replay, DRCPT-PG achieves the highest mean Sharpe ratio and the lowest mean maximum drawdown, turnover, and transaction cost among the five policy-gradient methods.  The pattern is economically coherent with the model: relative to fixed or symmetric references, asymmetric reference adaptation changes both risk exposure and trading intensity under the same point-in-time information and execution protocol.  Held-out investor data provide a complementary behavioral test.  Reference-based specifications improve out-of-sample prediction, and adaptive and asymmetric representations add further predictive information while sharpening the gain--loss partition associated with observed reductions.  The calibrated heterogeneous-agent replay then extends this comparison to repeated recommendation interactions, where adoption rates are similar across advisers but post-adoption interaction values favor DRCPT-PG across all four investor types.

Taken together, the evidence suggests a division of roles that is useful beyond this particular implementation.  The reference recursion determines how realized experience reshapes the behavioral objective; the gradient estimator supplies a reliable local first-order signal for that objective; and the online update determines how closely the policy follows the resulting moving target.  This view helps explain why the reference mechanism and online adaptation are complementary rather than interchangeable.  E4 shows that online updating remains useful under static and symmetric references, while gain-dominant asymmetry strengthens the tracking advantage.  The behavioral state therefore structures the target being followed, and the optimizer governs how effectively the learner follows it.

Several open questions follow from this framework.  Richer debiasing schemes may reduce the trajectory cost of the frozen-target oracle while retaining exactness, and sharper objective-sensitivity conditions may extend the online guarantees to more abrupt market and reference shifts.  On the behavioral side, a prospective human-in-the-loop study could jointly observe reference adaptation, recommendation uptake, realized portfolio outcomes, and reported utility under a common protocol, providing a direct test of how the modeled reference state maps to experienced investor preferences.  Taken together, the framework turns adaptive reference formation from a behavioral specification into an optimization state that can be estimated, tracked, and evaluated within a unified first-order portfolio-learning framework.
