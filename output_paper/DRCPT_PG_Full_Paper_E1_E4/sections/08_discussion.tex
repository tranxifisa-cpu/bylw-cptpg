\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The results support one central conclusion: once the reference point is treated as an endogenous state, CPT portfolio learning becomes a coupled behavioral, statistical, and online-optimization problem.  E1 shows why the state is consequential---matched accounts can rank the same feasible portfolios differently solely because their wealth histories imply different references.  E2--E4 then show that the corresponding first-order target can be estimated with controlled statistical error and tracked under a moving objective.  E5--E7 carry the same mechanism into chronological market data, held-out investor decisions, and heterogeneous interaction without replacing the theorem-aligned diagnostics by downstream performance measures.

Relative to fixed-reference CPT policy-gradient optimization, the main technical change is therefore not simply an additional state variable.  Endogenous reference adaptation makes the objective path dependent and moving, while nonlinear probability weighting makes its frozen-target gradient statistically nontrivial.  The centered leave-one-out base and independent randomized correction address the latter by producing exact unbiasedness under the complete-trajectory oracle, and the projected-residual analysis addresses the former by replacing single-point convergence with first-order tracking.  The estimator diagnostics, slow-variation experiment, and E4 reference-mechanism ablation with horizon and optimizer robustness checks jointly support this separation: centering, trajectory-budget scaling, online residual accumulation, mechanism strength, and implementation sensitivity are measured on the objects that appear in the theory.

The external-validity layers reveal a complementary pattern.  In the real A-share replay, DRCPT-PG has the highest mean Sharpe ratio and the lowest mean drawdown, turnover, and transaction cost across the five policy-gradient methods.  In the investor data, the predictive improvement is hierarchical: introducing a reference produces the main gain, while adaptive and asymmetric updating provide further resolved improvements and enlarge the reference-conditioned disposition gap.  The heterogeneous-agent replay then separates uptake from value after uptake.  Adoption rates are similar across methods, yet DRCPT-PG ranks first on adoption-weighted interaction value in every calibrated investor type, indicating that the separation is driven by the quality of the post-adoption outcome rather than by easier acceptance alone.  These three layers connect dynamic reference formation to market risk allocation, observed decision behavior, and repeated recommendation interaction while preserving distinct economic and behavioral estimands.

The evidence map also defines the scope of the claims.  The estimator and tracking theorems concern the regularized frozen-target gradient and a slowly varying objective sequence under the stated assumptions.  E5 evaluates chronological market transfer with quote-relative returns and common trading costs; E6 is a held-out predictive comparison rather than a causal identification design; and E7 is a calibrated counterfactual interaction assay rather than observed user adoption or satisfaction.  Keeping these layers separate prevents portfolio returns, predictive fit, and simulated interaction value from becoming interchangeable proxies for first-order optimization quality.  A natural next step is a prospective human-in-the-loop evaluation in which reference adaptation, recommendation uptake, realized portfolio outcomes, and user-reported utility are collected under a common pre-registered protocol.
