\section{Related Work and Positioning}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{CPT optimization and reinforcement learning.}
Prospect theory and \CPT provide the behavioral foundations for reference-dependent valuation and nonlinear probability weighting \citep{kahneman1979prospect,tversky1992advances}.  Their use in sequential learning began with \CPT prediction and control in reinforcement learning \citep{prashanth2016cumulative}, while recent work has made the optimization side substantially more explicit.  \citet{lepel2026policygradients} derive a first-order policy-gradient theorem for finite-horizon \CPT-RL, together with an order-statistics Monte Carlo estimator and convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for approximate stationarity.  Static and portfolio-specific \CPT optimization has also advanced through convex-analytic and splitting-based formulations \citep{luxenberg2024portfolio,cui2025decision,wu2026behavioral}.  These studies establish that \CPT objectives can be optimized despite their nonconvex and distribution-dependent structure.  Our setting departs from them at the level of the target itself: realized wealth changes the reference state, so the policy faces a path-dependent sequence of \CPT objectives rather than repeated optimization of one fixed reference-dependent criterion.

\paragraph{Adaptive references, path dependence, and investor behavior.}
The empirical motivation for an evolving reference predates the recent optimization literature.  Security-trading experiments document reference-point adaptation after gains and losses, including asymmetric adjustment \citep{arkes2008reference}.  Subsequent models show that adaptive references can alter realization behavior \citep{he2019realization}, while path-dependent references make current decisions depend on the route by which wealth reaches its present level \citep{qin2022realization}.  More recent portfolio analysis embeds dynamic reference mechanisms in multi-period allocation \citep{gao2024multiperiod}; complementary evidence shows that disposition behavior depends on portfolio-wide gain--loss frames \citep{an2024portfolio} and, for Chinese retail investors, on the information environment and investor characteristics \citep{chen2025xueqiu}.  Together, this literature explains why the reference should evolve with the realized portfolio path.  What it does not yet provide is a first-order \CPT policy-gradient framework whose gradient estimation and online tracking theory explicitly account for that endogenous reference evolution.

\paragraph{Nonstationary and nonconvex online optimization.}
When objectives are nonconvex and time varying, global comparator regret is often too strong to describe first-order learning.  Dynamic local regret instead evaluates stationarity after smoothing recent first-order information \citep{aydore2019dynamic}.  Recent work has sharpened this viewpoint through gradient-mapping criteria for online nonconvex composite optimization \citep{hallak2025sound} and projected-gradient analyses for constrained nonconvex and stochastic smooth problems \citep{lan2026projected}.  In nonstationary reinforcement learning, variation budgets likewise quantify how much environmental drift can be tolerated while retaining learnability \citep{feng2023nonstationary}.  Our problem shares the need for a local first-order criterion but differs in the origin of nonstationarity: part of the objective drift is generated by the learner's own wealth path and reference recursion.  We therefore work with the normalized projected residual of the contemporaneous \CPT objective and derive dynamic local regret from its temporally smoothed trajectory.

\paragraph{Debiasing and statistical guarantees for the gradient oracle.}
For a fixed reference, the \CPT policy gradient remains statistically unusual because the policy score is multiplied by a nonlinear functional of an unknown survival distribution.  The order-statistics estimator of \citet{lepel2026policygradients} is the closest first-order baseline and comes with consistency and optimization guarantees.  We instead ask for an exact finite-sample property of the frozen-target gradient oracle.  Building on randomized unbiased telescoping ideas \citep{rhee2015unbiased}, we combine a centered leave-one-out base with a randomized full/half correction and obtain exact unbiasedness together with finite expected trajectory cost and variance.  The same construction supports studentized asymptotic inference and a fixed-dimensional minimax trajectory-budget characterization, which makes the oracle directly usable inside the online tracking analysis.

\paragraph{Portfolio evaluation and investor-facing systems.}
The downstream experiments connect the theoretical mechanism to two empirical literatures.  Dirichlet factor policies provide a natural precedent for simplex-valued reinforced portfolios whose concentration parameters depend on firm characteristics \citep{andre2020dirichlet}, while recent financial-RL work emphasizes time-varying market environments \citep{liu2024dynamicdatasets}.  Evidence on Chinese A-shares also documents market-specific factor structure \citep{hanauer2024factor}, motivating a chronological point-in-time replay rather than a static random split.  A separate line of work studies persona-specific economic risk preferences in language models \citep{liu2025riskpreferences} and personalized generative-AI financial advisers \citep{takayanagi2025financialadvisors}.  Our behavioral evaluation complements these studies by asking whether dynamic reference states add information about held-out investor decisions and how reference-aware portfolio recommendations interact with heterogeneous investor profiles.
