\section{Related Work and Positioning}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{CPT optimization and reinforcement learning.}
Prospect theory and \CPT provide the behavioral foundations for reference-dependent valuation and nonlinear probability weighting \citep{kahneman1979prospect,tversky1992advances}.  Their use in sequential learning began with \CPT prediction and control in reinforcement learning \citep{prashanth2016cumulative}, while recent work has made the optimization side substantially more explicit.  \citet{lepel2026policygradients} derive a first-order policy-gradient theorem for finite-horizon \CPT-RL, together with an order-statistics Monte Carlo estimator and convergence and sample-complexity guarantees for approximate stationarity.  Static and portfolio-specific \CPT optimization has also advanced through convex-analytic and splitting-based formulations \citep{luxenberg2024portfolio,cui2025decision,wu2026behavioral}.  These works establish that \CPT objectives can be optimized despite their nonconvex and distribution-dependent structure.  The present paper changes the target rather than only the solver: realized wealth updates the reference state, so the policy faces a path-dependent sequence of \CPT objectives.  This creates both a finite-budget gradient-estimation problem at each frozen target and an online tracking problem across targets.

\paragraph{Adaptive references, path dependence, and investor behavior.}
The empirical motivation for an evolving reference is older than the optimization machinery but remains directly relevant.  Security-trading experiments document reference-point adaptation after gains and losses, including asymmetric adjustment \citep{arkes2008reference}.  Mathematical-finance models subsequently show that adaptive references can alter realization behavior \citep{he2019realization}, while path-dependent references make current decisions depend on the route by which wealth reaches its present level \citep{qin2022realization}.  More recent portfolio analysis embeds dynamic reference mechanisms in multi-period allocation \citep{gao2024multiperiod}; complementary evidence shows that disposition behavior depends on portfolio-wide gain--loss frames \citep{an2024portfolio} and, for Chinese retail investors, on the information environment and investor characteristics \citep{chen2025xueqiu}.  This literature establishes the behavioral mechanism that motivates our state variable, but it does not provide a first-order \CPT policy-gradient procedure whose estimator and tracking guarantees explicitly accommodate endogenous reference drift.

\paragraph{Nonstationary and nonconvex online optimization.}
When objectives are nonconvex and time varying, global comparator regret is often too strong to describe first-order learning.  Dynamic local regret instead measures stationarity after smoothing recent first-order information \citep{aydore2019dynamic}.  Recent work has sharpened this viewpoint through gradient-mapping criteria for online nonconvex composite optimization \citep{hallak2025sound} and through projected-gradient analyses for constrained nonconvex and stochastic smooth problems \citep{lan2026projected}.  In nonstationary reinforcement learning, variation budgets likewise quantify how much environmental drift can be tolerated while retaining learnability \citep{feng2023nonstationary}.  Our setting differs in the source of that drift: part of the objective movement is generated by the learner's own realized wealth and reference recursion.  We therefore smooth the normalized projected residual associated with the contemporaneous \CPT objective, obtain DLR from that residual, and keep objective variation and simulator error explicit in the online bound.

\paragraph{Debiasing and statistical guarantees for the gradient oracle.}
The frozen-target \CPT gradient is statistically unusual because the policy score is multiplied by a nonlinear functional of an unknown survival distribution.  The order-statistics estimator of \citet{lepel2026policygradients} provides the closest first-order baseline and consistency guarantees.  We target a different finite-sample property: exact unbiasedness for the regularized frozen-target gradient under a complete-trajectory oracle.  The randomized correction builds on unbiased telescoping ideas \citep{rhee2015unbiased}, but the substantive contribution is the complete oracle characterization needed by the online algorithm: finite expected cost and variance, studentized asymptotic inference, and a fixed-dimensional minimax trajectory-budget rate.  Classical empirical-process, central-limit, and decision-theoretic tools enter the proofs, but they are proof ingredients rather than separate research lines in the positioning of the paper.

\paragraph{Portfolio and human-facing external validity.}
The empirical layers use recent work only where it bears directly on the transfer being tested.  Dirichlet factor policies provide a natural precedent for simplex-valued reinforced portfolios whose concentration parameters depend on firm characteristics \citep{andre2020dirichlet}.  Recent financial-RL work emphasizes time-varying market environments \citep{liu2024dynamicdatasets}, while contemporary evidence on Chinese A-shares highlights market-specific factor structure \citep{hanauer2024factor}; these motivate the chronological, point-in-time E5 design rather than serving as substitutes for the controlled E2--E4 diagnostics.  The behavioral layers address a different transfer.  Recent studies test whether language models can represent persona-specific economic risk preferences \citep{liu2025riskpreferences} and whether personalized generative-AI financial advisers can elicit preferences and influence investment decisions \citep{takayanagi2025financialadvisors}.  E6 therefore asks whether the dynamic-reference representation carries incremental information about held-out investor decisions, whereas E7 asks how recommendations interact with heterogeneous investor types calibrated from those decisions.  The two tests are kept separate so that predictive behavioral alignment, recommendation uptake, and post-adoption value remain distinct empirical objects.
